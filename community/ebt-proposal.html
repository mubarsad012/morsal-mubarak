Nourish to Flourish

PHYAP Project by Morsal Mubarak · deck title: Nourish to Flourish: Empowering Communities through Food Education and EBT Access

I made this as my project for the Fairfax County Health Department's Public Health Youth Ambassador Program (PHYAP). It plans a one-week, after-school workshop for students about to leave high school who may face food insecurity in college. They learn how to apply for an EBT card, tell needs from wants, find food pantries, and shop and cook on a budget.

Overview

As students finish high school and get ready for college, the ones who face food insecurity run into a hard question: where will their next meal come from, and how will they get good meals every day? Many students can face food insecurity in college and may not know how to overcome it. My proposal is an EBT Education Workshop for young adults moving on to the next stage of their education.

An EBT card sets an amount of money that can be spent on food, and students can get one only if they apply and meet the requirements. The workshop would run for one week in the last month of the school year, after school, with a Zoom option for students who can't attend in person. It would give a step-by-step tutorial on applying for an EBT card, finding food pantries nearby, putting needs before wants, and shopping and cooking on a budget.

Why a workshop and not just a pantry

In the deck's speaker notes, I describe what already exists at Justice High School: the school partners with Food for Neighbors, which helps feed Justice students outside of school. The money raised and food collected created the Wolf Shack, where students go after school to pick up food, toiletries, or school supplies they need, and it is open to everyone. The notes call this a great idea, but point out that it might not be available to students once they graduate high school. Food pantries, food banks, and organizations are important, but the notes argue that students also need something stronger, like this EBT Education Workshop.

PHYAP, the program I made this for, is a free, grant-funded Fairfax County Health Department program that trains high school students in grades 10–12 in community health work.

My role. I wrote this proposal and its 15-slide deck. The title slide reads “PHYAP Project by Morsal Mubarak,” and neither the slides nor the speaker notes name other contributors. This page shows the plan as proposed. It does not report results from running the workshop.

Why it matters

The deck starts by explaining food insecurity for people new to the topic.

Hunger

Not having enough food for your body to produce the energy it needs to work properly. It is more than wanting to eat. It means being unable to eat because of food insecurity and a lack of food and nutrition, often from a lack of money or resources.

Homelessness

Facing hunger while also lacking the resources to get back on your feet, including shelter. It is harder to overcome because it takes resources for both shelter and food, and it usually starts with high housing costs and no stable job that pays enough.

What hunger does to health

  • Affects the immune system, so people are more likely to get sick
  • Longer-term effects include chronic diseases and disabilities that can lead to death
  • Weakens bones, muscles, and skin
  • Lowers energy and performance
  • A big increase in stunted growth
  • Depression
  • Anxiety
  • Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD)
  • Can lead to addictions: smoking, drugs, alcohol
  • Trouble staying focused
  • Withdrawing from others: feeling you don't fit into society, so you keep to yourself

Where each starting value comes from

  • Cookbooks. “Order around 35 cookbooks for students attending the fifth day. (Around $450)” gives $450 ÷ 35 = $12.857… per book, shown as $12.86.
  • Posters. “Print 50 colored posters with information about where, when, and what the workshop is about. (Around $15)” gives $15 ÷ 50 = $0.30 per poster.
  • Guest speaker. “Guest speaker will be paid around 50-100 dollars per day. They will only be there for 2 days” gives $100 to $200.
  • Meal ingredients. “Around 100 dollars will be used on the fifth day for the ingredients needed to cook the featured healthy meal.” The deck gives one figure for the meal; this calculator scales it with class size as an estimate, from the 35-student baseline: $100 ÷ 35 ≈ $2.86 per student.
  • Total. $450 + $100 + $15 + $100 = $665 at $50 a day, and $450 + $100 + $15 + $200 = $765 at $100 a day.
