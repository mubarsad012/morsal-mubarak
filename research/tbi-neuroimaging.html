Overview

Traumatic brain injury often leaves people with headaches, fatigue, poor concentration, and other symptoms that last for months, even when standard scans look normal. Magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) is an MRI technique that measures brain chemicals instead of anatomy: markers of neuron health (NAA), cell-membrane turnover (choline), energy (creatine), glial cells (myo-inositol), and the excitatory neurotransmitters glutamate and glutamine (Glx).

This two-arm treatment study measured those chemicals in several brain regions at baseline, 14 days, and 3 months, and tracked symptoms with the Rivermead Post-Concussion Symptoms Questionnaire (RPQ) through 6 months. The questions were whether brain chemistry changes differently between the treatment and control groups, and whether chemistry tracks symptoms.

Small clinical studies are easy to fool yourself with: a single unusual participant can create a "significant" result, and testing many regions and metabolites makes false positives likely. So I designed the pipeline around guardrails, including outlier and influence screening, multiple-comparison correction, and a full audit that re-runs everything from the raw data.

My role. Lead Computational Researcher: I built the analysis and reporting pipeline (about 4,800 lines of Python across three scripts) and delivered a verified report to the study's principal investigator.

Brain regions

Posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), corpus callosum (CC), thalamus, and periventricular white matter (pvWM), with left and right voxels analyzed separately as well as combined.

Measurements

NAA, choline, creatine, myo-inositol, Glx, and lactate, plus metabolite ratios (e.g. Cho/NAA, NAA/Cr), filtered by each scan's quality-control flags before analysis.

The pipeline

Seven stages, from a raw spreadsheet to a verified report. Select a stage to see what it does.

Ingest & quality control

  • Reads the raw MRS workbook (openpyxl, pandas) and the RPQ symptom table.
  • Applies each spectrum's existing QC flags: main metabolites must pass QC, and lactate is only used when fitted with high confidence.
  • All sheet names, session labels, and thresholds live in a JSON config, so the analysis can be re-run without editing code.

Merge & change scores

  • Standardizes participant IDs across files and attaches each participant's study arm, checking that no one has two assignments and no rows are duplicated.
  • Maps scan sessions to baseline, 14 days, and 3 months, then computes change scores (later minus baseline), so each person serves as their own reference.

Group comparisons

  • Between arms: Welch's t-test (no equal-variance assumption) and the Mann–Whitney U test (no normality assumption), with Cohen's d effect sizes and confidence intervals.
  • Within arms over time: paired t-tests and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, with Cohen's dz.
  • Built with SciPy and NumPy.

Objective outlier screens

  • Every major analysis is run twice: on all data, and again after an objective, pre-set screen.
  • Group comparisons: modified z-score screening (flag if |z| > 3.5).
  • Correlations and regressions: influence diagnostics, flagging a point if Cook's distance > 4/n or |studentized residual| > 3 (statsmodels). Try it below.
  • Results that only exist with a flagged point are reported separately instead of being presented as findings.

Linking brain chemistry to symptoms

  • Pearson and Spearman correlations between each metabolite and RPQ scores, at baseline and as changes over time.
  • Leave-one-out sensitivity: each correlation is recomputed with every participant removed in turn.
  • Optional age-adjusted baseline regressions (ordinary least squares in statsmodels) as a sensitivity check.

Correcting for many tests

  • Testing many regions × metabolites × timepoints makes some p < 0.05 likely by chance, so the pipeline computes Bonferroni and false-discovery-rate (Benjamini–Hochberg) adjusted p-values, and the report shows adjusted p-values beside the raw ones in every relevant table.
  • Findings are sorted into clearly separated tiers: nominally significant, trend-level, and results that do not survive screening, so nothing exploratory is presented as confirmed.

Verify, then report

  • The final run regenerated every number from the raw data.
  • A verification audit compares every saved table against a fresh recomputation, spot-checks the top rows independently, and documents which participants were included or excluded from each finding.
  • Outputs: publication-style figures (matplotlib, seaborn, Plotly + Kaleido), an interactive HTML report, and a printable PDF (ReportLab) for the principal investigator.
