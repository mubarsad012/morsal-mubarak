AI-Guided Diagnostics for Neurocysticercosis: A Systematic Review of ESM-2 + AlphaFold

Chensa Makin, Akansha Tiwari, and Morsal Mubarak (senior, last author)

Neurocysticercosis is the leading cause of acquired epilepsy worldwide, and confirming it still depends on CT or MRI scans that are expensive and scarce where the disease is most common. This review asks a narrower, testable question: can a protein language model (ESM-2) and AI-predicted protein structures (AlphaFold) pick out the antibody targets a cheap blood test would need? We screened the published evidence and found the idea is plausible — but nobody has tested it on the parasite itself.

Overview

A blood test needs a target: a small patch on a parasite protein that a patient's antibodies actually latch onto. That patch is called a B-cell epitope. For Taenia solium, the tapeworm behind neurocysticercosis (NCC), finding those patches experimentally is slow and expensive, and the parasite has almost no solved 3-D protein structures to work from.

Two recent tools change what is possible on a laptop. ESM-2 is a protein language model: trained on hundreds of millions of protein sequences, it turns a raw amino-acid sequence into a numerical representation that already encodes structural and functional information, without needing a known relative to copy from. AlphaFold predicts a protein's three-dimensional shape at close to experimental accuracy. This review asks whether putting the two together is a credible way to identify and rank diagnostic epitope candidates for NCC — and what the published evidence can and cannot support.

The short answer: every ingredient works on its own, and four independent tools show that combining sequence and structure beats either one alone. But the combination only helps when the system knows how much to trust the predicted structure, and no study has yet run the full pipeline on a T. solium antigen that is already used to diagnose patients. So the finding is feasibility is plausible, not yet established.

Why neurocysticercosis matters

2.5–8.3Mpeople affected globally, with up to 4.36 million cases directly linked to epilepsyManuscript, abstract
>80%of the global epilepsy burden falls on low- and middle-income countriesManuscript, abstract
0studies have applied the integrated ESM-2 + AlphaFold pipeline to a known T. solium diagnostic antigenManuscript, abstract & conclusion

Confirmatory diagnosis leans on neuroimaging, which is costly and scarce in endemic settings. Blood-based assays exist but are limited by cost, technical demands, and cross-reactivity with related tapeworm species. That bottleneck is the reason to care about epitope-guided tests: a synthetic peptide assay would not depend on sourcing live-parasite material for antigen production.

The question we set out to answer

The primary question was whether existing evidence supports using ESM-2-based protein representations together with AlphaFold-derived structural information to identify and prioritize diagnostic B-cell epitope candidates relevant to neurocysticercosis, with the goal of more accessible diagnostics in low- and middle-income countries. Four secondary questions organized the synthesis.

  1. 01How has ESM-2 been applied to B-cell epitope identification or prediction, and through what mechanism?
  2. 02What advantages and limitations do ESM-2-based approaches have relative to conventional sequence-based or structure-based methods?
  3. 03How far do these approaches apply to the known diagnostic antigens of Taenia solium and NCC?
  4. 04How could the approach support more accessible diagnostic tools in low- and middle-income countries?

A conventional PICO structure was not used: the included studies span protein language modeling, structural bioinformatics, immunoinformatics, and T. solium parasitology, with different outcome metrics and validation standards, so a meta-analysis would have compared things that are not comparable.

How the search and screening worked

Four search strands

One Boolean strand per component of the question, so a study that covered only one piece would still surface.

Six sources, 2016–2026

PubMed, Google Scholar, IEDB, Europe PMC, LILACS, plus bioRxiv for preprints. LILACS was limited to English-language records.

Two screening tracks

Rayyan for de-duplication and title/abstract screening; SciSpace for parallel discovery, keeping records scoring above 75/100 on its relevancy metric.

Quality appraisal

PROBAST for prediction-model studies, QUADAS-2 for diagnostic-accuracy studies. Citations managed in Zotero, APA style.

What each strand was looking for

ESM-2 and B-cell epitope prediction — computational feasibility. Does the protein language model help find epitopes at all?

ESM-2 and structural information — structural feasibility. Does adding predicted structure to the language model help, and how is it added?

Taenia solium / NCC and diagnostic epitopes — biological and diagnostic feasibility. What is already known about the antigens used to diagnose this disease?

Taenia solium and computational methods — research-gap identification. Has anyone already pointed these tools at this parasite?

The evidence, chart by chart

Read these as a map of other people's results. Every number below was reported by a study included in the review. We ran no new experiments and trained no models; the contribution is the systematic search, the appraisal, and the synthesis.

BepiPred-3.0: epitope-prediction AUC by how the sequence is encoded

Swapping classical encodings for ESM-2 embeddings raised AUC from 0.63–0.64 to 0.762 (p < 0.001).

GraphBepi: AUC from each feature type on its own

ESM-2 alone outperformed both the evolutionary profiles and the structural descriptors it replaced.

AUC is the chance that the model ranks a true epitope residue above a non-epitope one. 0.5 is a coin flip, so the bars start there.

AlphaFold-predicted structures vs. experimental structures vs. no structure

Predicted structures land within about 0.01 AUC of experimentally solved ones in both tools — which is what makes this approach possible for a parasite with almost no solved structures.

DiscoTope-3.0 reported the same pattern a third way: moving from solved structures to AlphaFold2 (ColabFold) models cost only 0.6 of a percentile point.

Four tools that ablated their own inputs: sequence only, structure only, or both

In all four, the combination beat either input on its own — the single most consistent finding in the review.

EpiCluster went furthest: 83 hand-crafted structural features lifted AUC from 0.64 to 0.71, and adding a spatial clustering layer that forces predictions into contiguous surface patches raised it to 0.89 — a bigger gain than the structural features themselves.

AlphaEpi: AUC by fusion strategy, against the ESM-2-only baseline

The dashed line is ESM-2 alone (0.751–0.753). Naive fusion scored below it; only the confidence-aware dynamic selector beat it.

For context from the same study: AlphaFold3 structure alone reached only 0.619–0.620, and ESM-2 alone 0.751–0.753. Integration is conditional, not automatic — which is the caveat that drives the review's conclusion.

Confidence gates the whole thing

DiscoTope-3.0 loses roughly 5 percentile points for every 10-point drop in pLDDT, AlphaFold's own confidence score.Manuscript, p. 16

Low-confidence models cost accuracy

GraphBepi's F1 score nearly doubles between high- and low-confidence structures: 0.406 vs. 0.241.Manuscript, p. 16

Filtering rescues raw predictions

Raw AlphaFold3 co-folding gave acceptable docking geometry for only about 37% of antibody-antigen pairs; Rosetta-based filtering (AFilter) reached 96–98% reliability.Manuscript, p. 16 & Table 2

The targets this would have to work on

This is the part that makes the method worth testing on NCC specifically. Over 90% of natural B-cell epitopes are conformational — they only exist when the protein is folded, and they stop working the moment it is denatured. And every validated T. solium diagnostic epitope identified so far is conformational, not linear: T24/rT24H, Ts8B2, GP50, sTs14, TsF78, and TsP62/TsC28. Conformational epitopes are exactly the class that sequence-only methods handle worst and that structure-aware methods are built for.

Reported sensitivity and specificity of validated NCC diagnostic antigens

These are the benchmarks any computationally designed test would be measured against.

T24H / rT24H

94% sensitivity (101/107) in patients with two or more viable cysts, 98% specificity — but 63% (10/16) in single-cyst disease. Its epitope is disulfide-dependent: chemically reducing the protein abolishes antibody recognition entirely.Hancock et al., 2006, as reported in the manuscript, p. 20 & Table 1

Ts8B2 / CyDA

91.3% sensitivity in active NCC, 92.5–97.8% specificity, falling to 68.6% sensitivity in inactive disease. Synthetic linear peptides of this antigen were not recognized at all.Ferrer et al., 2012, as reported in the manuscript, p. 20 & Table 1

Three-antigen MAPIA panel

Combining rGP50, rT24H and sTs14 detected 97.5% of parenchymal and 100% of subarachnoid cases at 98.5% specificity.Toribio et al., 2023, as reported in the manuscript, p. 20

Two studies came close, then stopped

Only two included studies touched T. solium with any part of this toolkit. Muñoz et al. (2025) screened 586 T. solium sequences with BepiPred-2.0 and SignalP 5.0 to prioritize 22 antigenic proteins and confirmed two by Western blot — but that work predates ESM-2 and was never validated against patient serum. Sánchez-Juárez et al. (2025) used AlphaFold2 to build a validated homology model of a T. solium glutathione S-transferase, which demonstrates structural modeling is feasible but does no epitope identification. Neither integrated the two, and neither tested a diagnostic antigen.

What the evidence cannot carry

Practical limits on the method itself

  • Length ceiling. ESM-2 truncates sequences beyond 1,023 residues, so longer antigens cannot be embedded whole.
  • Compute cost. Generating embeddings takes 14.34 seconds per protein, against 0.08 seconds for random embeddings.
  • Precision stays modest. Favorable AUC hides weak precision in practice: EpiTune reported precision of 0.36, and CALIBER PR-AUC of 0.12–0.38. A ranked shortlist still needs wet-lab confirmation.
  • Access. AlphaFold3 is not open-source and enforces daily request limits, which constrains the throughput of any pipeline built on it.
  • Epitope-type dependence. The advantage concentrates on conformational epitopes; for linear epitopes CALIBER found randomly initialized embeddings beating ESM-2 (0.789 vs. 0.768) at over 170-fold less computation.

The verdict

The proposed ESM-2 + AlphaFold integration is computationally coherent and biologically justified for NCC epitope discovery: all validated diagnostic antigens are conformational, which is precisely the structural class this method addresses, and the approach sidesteps the absence of experimental T. solium structures. The evidence for each component is consistent across independent tools.

Feasibility is plausible rather than established. No included study has tested this pipeline directly against T. solium's own diagnostic antigens. The honest next step is the obvious one: run the integrated pipeline on an antigen such as T24H, where the ground truth is already known, and see whether it recovers the epitope that two decades of serology found the hard way.
